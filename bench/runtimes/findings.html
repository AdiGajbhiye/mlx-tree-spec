What the numbers say

  • A draft model roughly doubles decode on MLX enginesmlx-dspark with RedHat's DSpark draft reaches 57–60 tok/s up to 4k context, against 26 without a draft. MTPLX with MTP gets Swift 4-bit to 53–54, against 25.
  • Drafts lose ground as context growsAt 32k the MLX draft setups fall to 36–40 tok/s, a 1.6–1.8× gain instead of the 2.0–2.5× they get on short code prompts. MTPLX + MTP holds up best at 32k (39.2), just ahead of DSpark (37.2).
  • DSpark beat DFlash2 on the same engineOn mlx-dspark, the DSpark draft was faster than DFlash2 at every context length and on 5 of 6 tasks, and tied on math. DFlash2's drafts are accepted slightly more often, but DSpark costs less per round.
  • llama.cpp MTP barely helps IQ3_SMTP adds 16–37% on code, math, JSON and edits but is slower on prose (0.94×) and creative writing (0.82×). Without a draft, IQ3_S is the fastest decoder here (27.6 tok/s) because its weights are smallest.
  • Prefill: mlx-dspark leads, llama.cpp trailsAbout 800 tok/s at 4–8k on mlx-dspark, 760–780 on MTPLX, 570 on llama.cpp. A 32k prompt takes 46 s to first token on mlx-dspark and 67 s on llama.cpp. Drafts don't change prefill.
  • Lithos doesn't run well on the 32-core chiplithos-metal has no tuned setup for this GPU. It decodes at 17.5 tok/s without a draft, but prefill is about 3 tok/s, and DSpark makes decode slower, not faster (5–15 tok/s on most tasks).
  • MXFP4 vs 4-bit Swift: same without MTPThe AX MXFP4 and ukisai 4-bit models decode at the same speed without a draft (25.1 vs 24.8). With MTP the ukisai weights are 13–17% faster on the task mix, though nearly even on the 1k-context run.
  • Context copy only matters for editsMTPLX's default prompt-copy drafting takes the file-edit task from 69 to 158 tok/s. On every other task it makes no measurable difference.
